\section{Thermal model: validation, capacity and actuation interval}
\label{sec:s-model}

\paragraph{Validation literature.} Bacher and Madsen (Energy and Buildings
43, 2011, 1511--1522) identified grey-box RC models of a measured building by
forward selection with likelihood-ratio tests; they rejected the single-state
model, whose residuals were not white, and selected a four-state model. The
single node used here follows their control-oriented framework but not their
selected order. Shi et al.\ (2022) used an
RC network for multi-zone pre-cooling optimisation, and Wang et al.\ (2021)
validated a second-order thermal network for residential buildings (both in the
main text's reference list). The main text's
results are therefore reported with the capacity sweeps of Table~S1(b) and of
the main text's sensitivity table, not on the strength of a validated single
node.

\paragraph{Capacity.} At an hourly interval, capacities of $77.2$ (air mass),
$500$ and $1{,}200\,\text{kJ/K}$ give per-interval excursions of $93.3$, $14.4$
and $6.00\,^\circ$C and violate the main text's actuation condition, so a sweep there would compare
configurations that admit no comfort-feasible schedule. Table~S1(b) spans the
capacities around the reference at $\Delta t=15\,$min and marks each by the
two-sided condition (8) of the main text: its lower limit needs
$\Cth\ge\Qhvac\Delta t/\deadband=1{,}800\,\text{kJ/K}$, and its upper limit,
with the one-interval warming excursion at the $690\,$W peak load, is
$0.99\,^\circ$C at $2{,}400\,\text{kJ/K}$, so THERM's $1\,^\circ$C deadband
fails it narrowly there. THERM's comfort record is reported in every row so
that an infeasible configuration cannot be mistaken for a cheap one. The headline $4{,}800\,\text{kJ/K}$ is $50\,\text{kJ}\,
\text{m}^{-2}\text{K}^{-1}$ over $96\,\text{m}^2$ of participating surface,
which gives a time constant of $56\,$h and a free-float time across the $2\,$K
band of $4.3\,$h at the ninety-fifth percentile of seasonal load and $8.0\,$h
at the median. ISO~13786's areal capacity is a $24\,$h quantity; the capacity
effective at the cycle period is lower.

\paragraph{Actuation interval.} With the exact update the interval carries no
integration error; it decides how often a controller may act and whether THERM
can hold its own deadband. THERM begins to leak at $30\,$min and fails at
$1\,$h, where the two arms are no longer held to the same constraint. The
saving is \emph{not} independent of the interval. A finer interval lets the
tuned thermostat widen its deadband (to $1.78$--$1.79\,^\circ$C at $5\,$min,
against $0.94$--$0.99\,^\circ$C at $30\,$min) while THERM keeps its fixed one
(Table~S1(a)). TUNED saves about $25\%$ at $5\,$min and $-1.2$ to $-1.5\%$ at
$30\,$min (main text, Section~9.3 and Table~8), where THERM's deadband fails
the upper limit of (8) narrowly ($0.99\,^\circ$C) and THERM leaks. Comparisons
with studies at a different interval must take that into account.

\paragraph{Stability and the discrete tariff.} Under the exact update the
one-step map is $e^{\mathbf{M}\Delta t}$, with spectral radius
$\exp(-\Delta t\,\mu_{\min}/\Cth)<1$ for every $\Delta t>0$, where $\mu_{\min}$
is the smallest eigenvalue of $\operatorname{diag}\Lambda+L_{\mathrm{iz}}$.
Forward Euler has map $I+\Delta t\mathbf{M}$ and requires
$\Delta t<2\Cth/\mu_{\max}$. At $\Delta t=1\,$h, with the conductances of the
main text's Table~2 and $\lij=12.5\,$W/K, that is $\Cth>40.8$--$44.3\,\text{kJ/K}$
for the single zone and $\Cth>85.8$--$185.1\,\text{kJ/K}$ for the coupled
topologies ($1\times2$ to $4\times4$, three cities); the air-only capacity of
$77.2\,\text{kJ/K}$ meets it for the single zone and violates it for every
coupled topology. The scheduler acts on a grid, so the realised bill is
$\sum_t p(E_{\mathrm{cum}}^{(t)})\Delta E^{(t)}$, which equals the integral of
the main text's path proposition only when no tier boundary falls strictly
inside an interval's increment; where one does, the discrete bill is
path-dependent at the order of one interval's consumption ($0.17\,$kWh here,
against tier widths of $2{,}000\,$kWh). For the account modelled no boundary is
approached, so the discrete and continuous statements coincide.

\section{The SLHA abstraction and the corollary's mean}
\label{sec:s-slha}

An SLHA requires a constant flow in each mode, whereas the rates of
the main text's SLHA definition depend on $T$ through $\Lambda(\Tout-T)$. Reading them as
constants evaluates them at a fixed reference temperature. With
$\Lambda=23.75\,$W/K and $\Cth=4{,}800\,$kJ/K ($\tau=56.1\,$h) the exact
free-floating rise time $\tau\ln[(\Tout-\Tmin)/(\Tout-\Tmax)]$ across the band
is $9.378$, $6.612$ and $5.107\,$h at outdoor temperatures of $35$, $40$ and
$45\,^\circ$C, against $9.356$, $6.604$ and $5.103\,$h from the constant-rate
expression, errors of $0.2\%$, $0.1\%$ and $0.1\%$. A wider band, a smaller
capacity or a leakier envelope would widen the gap.

Under constant drift each linear phase of a cycle averages to the midpoint of
its band, so the maximal-width cycle attains the mean
$(T^{g}_{\mathrm{on}}+T^{g}_{\mathrm{off}})/2$ and no other. On a Riyadh
held-out week's mean disturbance with guards at $23.60/22.40\,^\circ$C,
exponential rather than linear drift moves the cycle mean $0.006\,$K from the
midpoint. The $15$-min step matters more: a switch occurs at the first sample
past a threshold, so the zone overshoots the off threshold by up to one
interval's cooling ($0.29\,$K here) and the sampled cycle mean lies $0.09\,$K
below the midpoint. Matching is therefore done on the
simulated trajectory, never on the midpoint formula.

\section{Orientation and actual weather years}
\label{sec:s-amy}

The headline zone's glazed face is south-facing. The full protocol is repeated
in Riyadh with the glazed face turned to each cardinal direction, envelope,
plant and windows unchanged: TUNED saves $13.3$, $12.7$, $12.5$ and $12.2\%$
facing north, east, south and west, all fitted at pooled $0.03\,$K.

A typical year is not any year that occurred, so fifteen actual years per city
(2009--2023, ERA5 reanalysis on a $0.25^\circ$ cell, shifted by one hour to
align its timestamps with the TMYx convention, with the same plane-of-array
transposition) are used to measure spread at fixed method and plant. From
these the mildest, median and hottest years by cooling degree-days at base
$24\,^\circ$C are run in two ways (ERA5 is a reanalysis, not the station record
behind TMYx, and some of these years fall within the TMYx period of record, so
the spread mixes year-to-year variation with dataset differences): \emph{transfer} applies the TMYx-tuned parameters unchanged, as a
thermostat commissioned once would experience, and \emph{re-tuned} repeats
Rule~A inside the year. TUNED saves (transfer / re-tuned, \%; years in the
order mildest, median, hottest):
Riyadh 2013 $12.4/14.4$, 2019 $12.0/13.8$, 2021 $12.8/11.5$; Jeddah 2011
$13.2/14.5$, 2015 $13.4/12.3$, 2023 $13.4/14.8$; Taif 2013 $10.5/14.3$, 2018
$12.0/13.7$, 2017 $11.9/14.4$, all at pooled $0.03\,$K and with zero
over-temperature. In Taif's mild years 2013 and 2018 the zone free-floats below
the band for $6.7$--$6.9$ and $2.6$--$3.0\,^\circ$C$\cdot$h. The TMYx figures remain the
reported ones.

\section{Latent load}
\label{sec:s-latent}

A two-state zone model (sensible temperature and moisture, with hygric
buffering and a sensible-heat-ratio-limited coil) was built on the headline
parameters to test the sensible-only idealisation. Its coil did not reduce
sensible capacity when removing moisture, it placed the whole dwelling's
infiltration moisture in one room, and its tuning did not follow the protocol
of the main text. No result from it is reported, and the latent axis is
treated as untested. A coil with a capacity map and a per-room moisture
balance would be needed before any latent comparison.

\section{Multi-zone dwellings}
\label{sec:s-mz}

Riyadh, independent per-room units, the protocol of the main text applied to
the dwelling's mean occupied temperature, $\lij=12.5\,$W/K with $35\,$W/K as a
sensitivity. MPC-MZ is a non-cooperative distributed receding-horizon
controller with one-step-delayed plan exchange: each room solves its own
single-zone programme against its neighbours' previously announced plans and
minimises its own cost. It has no optimality guarantee, and no bound exists for
these layouts.
